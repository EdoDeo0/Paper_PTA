\begin{abstract}
\noindent Environmental provisions (EPs) are now ubiquitous in preferential trade agreements
(PTAs), yet evidence on their trade effects remains scarce and is based on aggregate data.
I study whether the EPs contained in the PTAs signed by China between 2000 and 2015 changed
the \emph{composition} of Chinese exports --- shifting firms' export baskets toward
environmental (green) goods and away from pollution-intensive (dirty) goods --- using
transaction-level customs data covering 45.8 million firm--product--destination--year
observations. Identification exploits a triple-difference design: within a given
firm--destination--year, I compare green and dirty products to neutral ones as EP depth
varies across destinations and over time, absorbing the confounding effect of the agreement
itself through firm--destination--year fixed effects. I find a precisely estimated null on the green margin, stable across six estimation
designs (full panel, collapsed panel, and four control-group subsamples), surviving wild
cluster bootstrap and permutation inference. The dirty margin is a more mixed picture: a
negative effect on dirty exports survives a wild cluster bootstrap ($p=0.07$) but the
finer-grained permutation test still finds it unusual ($p=0.02$), while a coarser
aggregation of the same test reverses its sign; a leave-one-out exercise shows it is
carried by a single destination (Australia), with South Korea, previously the pivotal
case, now only borderline (removing it leaves $p=0.09$). The central reading is not that environmental provisions are
inert, but that Chinese agreements bundle provisions so tightly that the two World Bank
sub-indices with a direct trade mechanism (green-goods liberalization, binding standards)
are perfectly collinear in-sample and non-zero in only three treated country--years:
mechanism-bearing content is essentially absent from Chinese PTAs of this period, and the
data cannot separately identify what it would do. This is consistent with, not a contrast
to, \citet{brandi2020}: their effects are driven by explicitly trade-restrictive or
trade-liberalizing clauses, and China's 2000--2015 agreements --- dominated by cooperation
language --- contain almost none of that content. The result is compatible with
\citet{abman2024} on the same logic: specific, enforceable provisions can move outcomes;
generic ones do not.\\[4pt]
\textbf{Keywords:} trade agreements, environmental provisions, export composition, China.\\
\textbf{JEL:} F13, F14, F18, Q56.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Nearly every trade agreement signed in the last two decades contains environmental
language. The TREND database \citep{morin2018} codes roughly 300 distinct types of
environmental provisions (EPs) across more than 700 preferential trade agreements (PTAs),
and the depth of this content has grown steadily. Whether these provisions have real
effects --- on trade flows or on environmental outcomes --- is a question that the
authoritative survey of the trade--environment literature flags as open
\citep{copeland2022}. Two recent papers provide the sharpest evidence to date.
\citet{brandi2020} combine TREND with aggregate bilateral trade flows and find that
trade-restrictive EPs reduce the dirty share, and liberal EPs raise the green share, of
developing-country exports. \citet{abman2024} show that forest- and biodiversity-specific
provisions fully offset the deforestation that otherwise follows entry into force of a
regional trade agreement.

This paper brings the question to the world's largest exporter with the most granular data
available: the universe of Chinese customs transactions, observed at the
firm--product(HS6)--destination--year level (49.2 million observations, roughly 462{,}000
exporting firms). Between 2000 and 2015 China signed around 14 PTAs --- covering 25
destination economies --- whose environmental content ranges from essentially none (the
Bangkok Agreement) to substantial chapters (New Zealand 2008, Switzerland 2014, Korea
2015). I ask whether deeper environmental content shifted the \emph{composition} of
Chinese exports toward green goods and away from dirty goods, relative to neutral
products, within the same firm, destination, and year.

The focus on composition, rather than on export volumes, is dictated by identification.
EP depth in Chinese agreements varies at the destination--year level, enters in force
together with the agreement itself, and afterwards almost never changes (only three
destinations exhibit within-country variation). Any level effect of EP depth on trade is
therefore observationally equivalent to the effect of the agreement, and of agreement
depth in general --- a collinearity that no fixed-effects structure can undo. I document
this formally with a saturation ladder: coefficients on EP depth shrink monotonically to
a precise zero as fixed effects absorb the level of the trade relationship, the signature
of selection into agreements rather than of a causal effect
\citep{bertrand2004, abadie2023}. The composition question, by contrast, is identified:
within a firm--destination--year cell, the agreement affects green, dirty, and neutral
products symmetrically, so their \emph{differential} response to EP depth isolates the
environmental content. The design follows the logic of the classic
industry-$\times$-country interaction of \citet{rajan1998}, and mirrors the ``content
conditional on agreement'' contrast that \citet{abman2024} identify as the credible
margin. The analogy is structural, not literal: \citeauthor{rajan1998}'s industries are
sorted by a technological trait (external finance dependence) that is fixed across
countries, whereas green and dirty status here is a \emph{policy}-defined product
classification (OECD and Mani--Wheeler lists), a distinction worth flagging even though
it does not vary with the agreement and so cannot itself confound identification.

The answer is a precisely estimated null, on both margins. Six independent estimation
designs --- the full firm-level panel, a product--destination--year collapsed panel, and
four control-group subsamples that probe the stability of the coefficient across
comparison sets --- return an EP$\times$green coefficient between $-0.0009$ and
$-0.0046$ (in log points per provision), never remotely significant, with standard errors
around 0.003--0.007. The dynamic version shows flat differential pre-trends and no break
at entry into force. Inference is conducted at three levels --- destination-clustered
asymptotics, wild cluster bootstrap \citep{cameron2008}, and permutation tests that
reshuffle entire EP profiles across treated destinations \citep{fisher1935,bertrand2004}
--- because only 23 destinations (and effectively 14 agreements) are treated. A
negative coefficient on EP$\times$dirty (asymptotic $p<0.001$ in the collapsed
panel) illustrates exactly why this discipline matters, though here the picture is more
mixed than on the green margin: its wild-bootstrap p-value is 0.07, permutation
inference on the exact estimating equation still finds it unusual ($p=0.02$), a coarser
aggregate version of the same permutation flips its sign ($p=0.49$), the TREND index never
confirms it, and a leave-one-out exercise shows it is carried by a single destination
(Australia, $p=0.24$ once excluded); South Korea, the destination whose removal
previously killed the result, now leaves it only borderline ($p=0.09$). The joint
F-test on all four composition interactions in the full firm-level panel is $p=0.31$ (WB
index) and $p=0.71$ (TREND index).

The null is informative rather than disappointing, and its central reading is about
\emph{content}, not about environmental provisions in general. Decomposing the indices
reveals that Chinese EPs of the period bundle provisions so tightly that the two World
Bank sub-indices with a direct trade mechanism --- green-goods liberalization and
environmental standards --- are \emph{perfectly} collinear in-sample ($\rho = 1.00$) and
non-zero in only three treated country--years; most environmental content is soft
cooperation language with no trade mechanism at all. Where \citet{brandi2020} find
effects, they are driven by explicitly trade-restrictive or trade-liberalizing clauses;
the Chinese agreements of 2000--2015 contained almost none of either. Read this way, the
null is not a contrast with \citet{brandi2020} but what their own heterogeneity predicts
for agreements of this composition --- and, on the same logic, compatible with
\citet{abman2024}'s finding that specific, enforceable provisions do move outcomes: China's
chapters were neither. Two further facts reinforce this reading. First, the estimate is
precise: obtained on the near-universe of transactions of the world's dominant exporter,
it firmly rejects economically meaningful composition effects (say, half the magnitudes
in \citealp{brandi2020}), so the null is not merely a failure to reject. Second, it is
stable across control groups --- an artifact of the comparison set would move as the
comparison set moves, and it does not.

This paper contributes to three literatures: the trade effects of PTA content
\citep{hofmann2017, dur2014, neri2023, baccini2017}, the trade--environment nexus
\citep{cherniwchan2017, shapiro2021, dechezlepretre2017}, and the effectiveness of
environmental clauses in trade agreements \citep{brandi2020, abman2024, morin2018,
baghdadi2013}.
Methodologically, it offers a template for honest inference when treatment varies across
few clusters \citep{cameron2008, abadie2023} and for control-group stability analysis in
triple-difference designs.

The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows. Section~\ref{sec:background} describes
the agreements and the data. Section~\ref{sec:strategy} presents the empirical strategy.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the main results, Section~\ref{sec:robust} the
robustness battery, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Background and data}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Chinese PTAs and their environmental content}

Between 2000 and 2015, PTAs signed by China and entering into force cover 25 destination
economies. Table~\ref{tab:treatment} lists them. Three features of this treatment
landscape discipline everything that follows. First, the ASEAN agreement (2005) covers
eleven destinations with identical values of every EP index: the effective number of
agreements is roughly 14, not 25. Second, EP content is fixed at signature: only three
destinations (notably South Korea, which moved from the shallow Bangkok Agreement to the
deep 2015 FTA) display within-country variation over time, and in no case does depth
ever fall --- there is no treatment reversal in the sample, so estimators built for
treatment switching on and off are not required here. Third, Hong Kong and Macao
(CEPA, 2003) are entrep\^ot economies whose recorded flows are dominated by re-exports;
they account for half of the export value to treated destinations and are excluded from
the main specification (robustness with inclusion reported below). Following
\citet{feenstra2004}, a large share of ``Chinese exports'' to Hong Kong are in practice
intermediated and re-exported to third countries at a substantial markup, so treating
Hong Kong--Macao as a genuine final destination would misattribute third-country demand
shocks to the bilateral relationship the fixed effects are meant to absorb.

EP content is measured with two independent codings: the World Bank Deep Trade

\subsection{Customs data and product classification}

The trade data are the universe of Chinese export customs transactions 2000--2015,
aggregated to the firm--HS6--destination--year level: 49.2 million observations
(45.8 million once Hong Kong and Macao are dropped), about 462{,}000 firms, roughly
5{,}000 HS6 products and 236 destinations. The outcome is the log of export value; the
panel identifiers used for fixed effects (firm--product--destination,
firm--destination--year, product--year) are precomputed.

\emph{Green} products are the 247 HS6 codes of the OECD Combined List of Environmental
Goods (CLEG) as compiled in \citet{sauvage2014} (248 codes; verified match of 246/248
against the project's source file, with the single discrepancy a granularity split of
one HS heading into two sub-codes). Because the customs panel uses the HS1996 vintage
while the OECD list is native HS2012, the list was translated to HS1996 (a unique 1:1
concordance exists for all 247
codes; export-value continuity checks at the 2002/2007/2012 revision boundaries confirm
a clean mapping). \emph{Dirty} products are the HS6 codes belonging to the classic
pollution-intensive sectors of \citet{mani1998} and \citet{low1992} --- pulp and paper,
industrial chemicals, petroleum refining, iron and steel, non-ferrous metals (plus
cement in an extended list) --- mapped to HS1996 through the official WITS/UNSD
HS1996--ISIC Rev.3 correspondence: 1{,}139 codes. Seventeen codes appearing in both
lists (e.g.\ insulation materials: produced by dirty industries, used for environmental
purposes) are assigned to the green list, which is product-curated; the two categories
are mutually exclusive. Green products account for 11.5\% of firm-level observations and
dirty products for 7.0\%; the remaining ``neutral'' products form the comparison group.

\subsection{Estimation samples and control-group subsamples}\label{sec:samples}

The analysis uses four estimation objects built from the same underlying panel, each
answering a different question. The \emph{full firm-level panel} (45.8 million
firm--HS6--destination--year observations once Hong Kong and Macao are dropped) is the
main object; its singleton-surviving estimation sample is characterized in
Section~\ref{sec:strategy}. The \emph{collapsed panel} aggregates it to
HS6--destination--year cells (3.77 million; outcome: within-cell mean of log exports,
regressions weighted by cell size) and serves both as a computational workhorse for
the inference battery and as an internal cross-validation --- its fixed-effects
structure replicates the identification argument one-for-one. The \emph{zero-filled
PPML grid} (8.3 million cells) adds the combinations with no recorded trade, opening
the extensive margin. The \emph{within-firm share panel} (13.3 million
firm--destination--year observations) recasts the outcome as the green share of each
firm's export basket to a destination, the margin aggregate data cannot see.

Because the triple-difference compares green and dirty products to \emph{neutral}
ones, the credibility of the comparison set can be probed directly. Four
control-group subsamples, in the spirit of the control-group batteries used in the
transaction-level trade-policy literature, each tighten the comparison in the
direction of a specific threat:

% ============================================================================
\section{Empirical strategy}\label{sec:strategy}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Why a level effect is not identifiable}

The natural first specification --- regressing log exports on EP depth with fixed
effects --- is uninformative here, and I document this as a diagnostic rather than as a
result. EP depth varies at the destination--year level and switches on with the
agreement; with $\sim$14 agreements and near-zero within-destination variation,
``environmental depth'' and ``having a (deep) agreement'' cannot be told apart. The
saturation ladder makes the point empirically: moving from sparse
(firm--product--destination + year) to saturated (adding product--time and pair-level
interactive effects) structures, the EP-depth coefficient falls monotonically from small
positive and starred to an exact, precisely estimated zero; the null in the most
saturated structure is confirmed by a wild cluster bootstrap (B=9{,}999; $p=0.97$ for
the WB index, $0.64$ for TREND, and $0.97$/$0.61$ with controls). The pattern ---
significance that lives only in under-saturated, finely-clustered specifications --- is
the classic signature of selection combined with understated standard errors
\citep{bertrand2004, goodmanbacon2021}. One consequence is worth stating plainly: this
design cannot supply the kind of first-stage ``bite'' evidence --- a demonstration that
signing an EP-laden PTA moved some intermediate outcome, such as aggregate trade
volumes, before composition is examined --- that would normally open a difference-in-
differences case, because that level effect is exactly what is confounded with the
agreement. The saturation ladder is offered as the closest available substitute: it
shows that whatever the agreement did at the level of trade, EP depth specifically is
not separably identified there, which is why the paper's evidence is built entirely on
the composition margin instead.

\subsection{The triple-difference on composition}

The parameter of interest is the average treatment effect on the treated of one
additional unit of environmental depth on the gap between green (or dirty) and neutral
log export value, within firm--destination--year cells that experience treatment: in
potential-outcomes terms, $\beta_1 = E[\,(y_{gpdt}(1) - y_{gpdt}(0)) - (y_{npdt}(1) -
y_{npdt}(0))\mid d,t \text{ treated}\,]$, where $y(1)$ and $y(0)$ denote log exports of
green ($g$) versus neutral ($n$) products under, respectively, deeper and unchanged
environmental content, holding the agreement itself fixed. This is a per-cell (per
firm--destination--year), not a per-firm, average: the collapsed-panel implementation
weights by cell size for the same reason, so that destinations or years with more
underlying transactions contribute proportionally more evidence, rather than every
HS6--destination--year cell counting equally regardless of how much trade it represents;
no weighting is by any post-treatment outcome. The main specification is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
\ln x_{fpdt} \;=\; \beta_1\, EP_{dt}\times g_p \;+\; \beta_2\, EP_{dt}\times b_p
\;+\; \gamma_1\, TD_{dt}\times g_p \;+\; \gamma_2\, TD_{dt}\times b_p
\;+\; \theta_{fpd} \;+\; \theta_{fdt} \;+\; \theta_{pt} \;+\; \varepsilon_{fpdt},
\end{equation}
where $f$ indexes firms, $p$ HS6 products, $d$ destinations, $t$ years; $g_p$ and $b_p$
are the green and dirty indicators, $EP_{dt}$ is environmental depth and $TD_{dt}$
non-environmental agreement depth. The firm--destination--year effects $\theta_{fdt}$
are the key: they absorb everything that varies at the level of the firm's relationship
with a market in a year --- including the agreement itself, market size, demand shocks,
and selection into agreements. Identification comes from comparing green (and dirty)
products to neutral ones \emph{within} the same firm--destination--year, as EP content
varies across destinations and over time; $\theta_{pt}$ absorbs global product-level
shocks (e.g.\ the world solar boom) and $\theta_{fpd}$ the level of specialization.
The $TD$ interactions separate environmental content from general agreement depth, the
same role the DESTA index \citep{dur2014} plays in \citet{brandi2020}; because
\texttt{TotalDepth} counts all non-environmental provisions rather than isolating
green-relevant ones (e.g.\ subsidy disciplines, technical-standard harmonization), it is
an imperfect proxy for the depth of agreement content that is genuinely orthogonal to
the environmental chapter, so residual correlation between $EP_{dt}$ and $TD_{dt}$ would
attenuate, not inflate, the composition estimates. That correlation is substantial and
should be stated plainly: across the 223 treated destination--years in the estimation
sample, WB EP depth and non-environmental TotalDepth correlate at $0.91$ in the raw data
and $0.96$ after destination and year demeaning (the variation the design actually
uses); the raw-data VIF of $5.8$ is somewhat higher than the maximum of $4.6$ that
\citet{brandi2020} report. The EP$\times$green coefficient is therefore identified from
the modest share of environmental-depth variation that is orthogonal to general
agreement depth --- a limitation that the clustered standard errors already price in
(it is one reason the WB confidence intervals are wider than the TREND ones: the TREND
count is far less collinear with TotalDepth, at $0.50$ raw and $0.85$ within). That two
codings with such different degrees of overlap with general depth return the same null
is itself reassuring: a result driven by the inability to separate environmental from
general content would be expected to differ across them. Standard errors are clustered
by destination, the level of treatment variation \citep{abadie2023}.

A further identification concern works in a conservative direction. If deeper
environmental chapters are more likely to be signed with destinations where consumer or
regulatory demand for green goods is \emph{already} rising --- a plausible selection
story --- then $EP_{dt}$ is positively correlated with an omitted destination-specific
green-demand trend, which would bias $\beta_1$ upward, toward finding a spurious
positive effect on green exports. Because the estimated $\beta_1$ is a precise zero
rather than positive, this channel implies the true effect (if negative or zero) is, if
anything, understated by the null: the null is a conservative, not a lenient, reading of
the data.

Two complementary implementations address the computational burden of three
high-dimensional fixed effects on 46 million rows. The full panel is estimated with
iterative singleton removal \citep{correia2017}, which eliminates 24.3 million
uninformative observations. The surviving sample is not a selected sliver: it retains
47\% of observations but 70\% of aggregate export value (the removed observations are
predominantly one-off, small flows), and the product composition is essentially
unchanged (green products are 11.5\% of observations before removal and 11.9\% after;
dirty products 7.0\% and 6.4\%). Within the estimation sample, the composition contrast
is identified in the treated firm--destination--year cells that contain both a green
(or dirty) and a neutral product: 26\% of treated cells for the green margin and 12\%
for the dirty margin, with a median of one green (dirty) product per identifying cell
and three products per treated cell overall --- a reminder that the estimates aggregate
many small within-firm contrasts rather than a few large ones. A collapsed panel at the HS6--destination--year level (3.77
million cells; outcome: within-cell mean of log exports; regressions weighted by cell
size; fixed effects $pd+dt+pt$), in the gravity-equation tradition of
\citet{headmayer2014}, replicates the identification argument one-for-one --- $dt$ absorbs
the agreement as $fdt$ does in the full panel --- at a fraction of the cost, and the two
give nearly identical answers, an internal cross-validation of both pipelines.

\subsection{Inference with few treated clusters}

There are 236 destination clusters but only 23 treated ones, and effectively 14
agreements. Asymptotic cluster-robust inference is unreliable in this regime, so every
headline estimate is subjected to three tests: (i) destination-clustered asymptotics;
(ii) wild cluster bootstrap with 9{,}999 replications \citep{cameron2008,
fischer2021, roodman2019} --- computed on Frisch--Waugh-residualized data (fixed effects
demeaned once, not re-estimated at each bootstrap draw), an approximation that treats
$pt$ as if it were nested within the destination cluster, which it is not, while $pd$
and $dt$ are; demeaned coefficients match the unrestricted \texttt{fixest} estimates
exactly. With only 23 \emph{treated} clusters among 236, the more demanding regime of
\citet{mackinnon2017} and \citet{conley2011} applies: standard wild bootstrap asymptotics
assume the number of treated clusters, not just clusters overall, grows large, which is
why (iii) permutation inference \citep{fisher1935} is run as an independent check rather
than a formality. Entire EP profiles
(depth and timing) are randomly reassigned 1{,}000 times across treated destinations,
asking whether the observed coefficient stands out among placebos --- the sharpest
answer to ``is it really the environmental content, or would any labelling of these
destinations produce this number?''. The dynamic specification is an event study of the
green (dirty) vs.\ neutral differential around entry into force, with never-treated
destinations as controls, endpoint bins declared, and a Sun--Abraham version
\citep{sun2021} run on the destination-level composition gap to address staggered-timing
cohort heterogeneity \citep{goodmanbacon2021, dechaisemartin2020, callaway2021}.

\subsection{The green margin: a stable, precise null}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the main estimates. In the full firm-level panel the
EP$\times$green coefficient is $-0.0022$ (s.e.\ 0.0039) with the WB index and $-0.0001$
(s.e.\ 0.0010) with TREND; the joint F-test on all four composition interactions is
$p=0.31$ and $p=0.71$ respectively. The collapsed panel returns $-0.0046$ (WB): the wild
cluster bootstrap p-value is 0.65, and permutation inference on the exact estimating
equation --- 1{,}000 reassignments of entire EP profiles across treated destinations
--- gives $p=0.74$: the observed coefficient is well within the bulk of the placebo
distribution.

To gauge magnitudes, it helps to benchmark the confidence intervals against the
literature's reference estimate. \citet{brandi2020} find that one \emph{liberal}
environmental provision raises the green share of developing-country exports by 0.4
percentage points, about $+17\%$ of the mean green share --- roughly $+0.16$ log points
of green relative to non-green exports per provision in the metric of
equation~\eqref{eq:main}. The full-panel 95\% confidence interval for EP$\times$green
is $[-0.0100, +0.0055]$ per WB provision (collapsed panel: $[-0.0182, +0.0091]$): the
upper bound is about one twenty-ninth of the Brandi-equivalent effect, so the design
rejects not just their point estimate but anything larger than a few percent of it. On
the dirty margin their trade-restrictive provisions reduce dirty exports by roughly 5\%
of the mean per provision ($\approx -0.05$ log points); even taking our asymptotic
full-panel point estimate at face value ($-0.0044$ per provision), the response is an
order of magnitude smaller. Both comparisons carry the same caveat in both directions:
Brandi et al.'s effects are identified from explicitly liberal or trade-restrictive
clauses, which are almost entirely absent from Chinese agreements of this period
(Section~\ref{sec:robust}) --- which is precisely the interpretation the paper
advances. In per-standard-deviation terms, a one-SD increase in WB EP depth across
treated destination--years ($\approx$ 2.7 provisions) moves green relative to neutral
exports by at most $-2.7\%$ to $+1.5\%$ at the 95\% bounds.

\subsection{Dynamics}

Figure~\ref{fig:es} plots the event study. Differential pre-trends are flat for both
green and dirty products, and nothing happens at entry into force. This is a
falsification check, not a proof: parallel trends is a statement about the unobserved
counterfactual path of treated destinations after treatment, which no pre-period test
can directly verify; a flat pre-trend rules out the most detectable violations (an
already-diverging composition gap) without establishing that the assumption holds
exactly thereafter. The late negative drift for green products (bin
$\geq$+5) is identified only by early cohorts (ASEAN 2005, Chile 2006, Pakistan 2007,
New Zealand 2008) and does not survive the Sun--Abraham estimator applied to the
destination-level composition gap: the aggregated ATT is $-0.044$ ($p=0.24$) for the
green gap and $+0.073$ ($p=0.28$) for the dirty gap --- consistent with cohort
heterogeneity rather than a treatment effect \citep{sun2021}. The full Sun--Abraham
event study is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:sunab}; one distant lead (the dirty gap
at $t=-6$) is individually significant, and the appendix shows through a cohort
decomposition that it reflects the idiosyncratic deviation of a single late-cohort
destination rather than a common pre-trend.

\subsection{The dirty margin: a fragile, not a false, positive}\label{sec:dirty}

The strongly significant WB$\times$dirty coefficient in the collapsed panel
($-0.0119$, asymptotic $p<0.001$) deserves its own subsection, because it is a
textbook illustration of why few-cluster inference matters --- though here, unlike the
result the paper's earlier draft reported on this margin, the robustness battery does
not cleanly dismantle it. The wild cluster bootstrap raises its p-value to 0.07, just
above the conventional 5\% threshold. Permutation inference on the exact estimating
equation is more skeptical of the null than the bootstrap: reshuffling entire EP
profiles across treated destinations, only 2.3\% of placebo draws produce a coefficient
as large in absolute value ($p=0.02$). A coarser permutation on a
destination--year--product-type aggregate, by contrast, reverses the sign entirely
(+0.005, $p=0.49$): aggregating to one observation per destination--year--product-type
collapses the within-cell heterogeneity across firms and product varieties that the
disaggregated coefficient exploits, so the two permutation tests are not answering the
same question --- the exact-specification version is the one that matches the estimating
equation actually reported in Table~\ref{tab:main}, but the coarse version's instability
to the level of aggregation is itself evidence of fragility. A leave-one-out exercise
across the 23 treated destinations resolves the tension only partially: the coefficient
is stable ($\approx -0.012$) except when Australia is dropped, in which case it falls to
$-0.0103$ with $p=0.24$. South Korea, the destination that drove the equivalent result in
an earlier version of this analysis, is no longer pivotal on its own: dropping it leaves
the coefficient at $-0.0097$ with $p=0.09$, borderline but not decisive. The TREND index
never shows the effect (bootstrap $p=0.86$). In
the full firm-level panel the coefficient is $-0.0044$ with an asymptotic $p$ of 0.052
--- just above conventional significance, the same order of magnitude as in the CEM
subsample, and consistent with a marginal signal that inference discipline narrows but,
on this margin, does not fully erase. The honest reading is that the dirty margin is
\emph{fragile} rather than a clean false positive: three of four robust-inference checks
(wild bootstrap, coarse permutation, and near-borderline leave-one-out on Korea) point
toward a null, while the exact-specification permutation and the destination
sensitivity to Australia keep it from being dismissed outright. Section~\ref{sec:robust}
shows this fragility is compounded by the near-total absence of trade-restrictive
content in Chinese agreements' dirty-relevant chapters, the same structural limitation
that governs the green margin.

\section{Robustness and extensions}\label{sec:robust}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Provision bundling and the limits of heterogeneity analysis}

A natural objection is that the aggregate index dilutes provisions with a trade
mechanism (green-goods liberalization; binding standards) with cooperation language that
has none. The objection is correct in principle --- and unanswerable in this setting,
which is itself a finding. What follows is a mechanism argument \emph{for the null}, not
evidence of a positive causal channel: it explains why no effect is detected, rather
than tracing how an effect would operate if one existed. Across the 223 treated country--year observations in the
estimation sample, the two WB sub-indices with a direct mechanism
(\texttt{GreenLiberalization} and \texttt{StandardsNonRegression}) are \emph{perfectly}
collinear ($\rho = 1.000$) --- and the reason is stark: both are non-zero in only three
country--years (Korea from 2015; Switzerland from 2014), always in the same 1:3
proportion. Mechanism-bearing provisions effectively exist in two Chinese agreements of
the period. Their interactions load identically in the regression (identical
t-statistics; coefficients in exact 3:1 ratio). TREND
sub-indices correlate between 0.5 and 0.9 with each other and with the total count.
With $\sim$14 agreements, provision-type heterogeneity is not separately identifiable
--- in sharp contrast with \citet{brandi2020}, whose 680-agreement panel allows it (they
report a maximum VIF of 4.6 across their provision-type variables; here, for the two
mechanism-bearing sub-indices, it is unbounded). What can be said: the
mechanism-bearing bundle shows the same pattern as the aggregate index --- green never
responds (green-liberalization $\times$ green: $p=0.90$; TREND green-market-access
$\times$ green: $p=0.38$), while the dirty interaction is marginally negative on
asymptotic p-values ($p=0.04$--$0.07$), the same fragile signal as the aggregate. The
placebo components with no trade mechanism (TREND soft provisions, regulatory-space
clauses) show nothing on either margin ($p \geq 0.27$), which is reassuring: a selection
story would light up precisely there. Enforcement provisions --- dispute-settlement
mechanisms attached to the environmental chapter, which condition whether any of the
above is binding rather than providing a trade mechanism themselves --- are likewise
null on both margins and both codings (WB \texttt{EnforcementDSM} $\times$ green:
$p=0.91$; $\times$ dirty: $p=0.90$; TREND \texttt{EnforcementDSM} $\times$ green:
$p=0.78$; $\times$ dirty: $p=0.71$).

This bundling result also reconciles the null with \citet{brandi2020}'s positive finding,
even though both papers draw on the same underlying TREND/World Bank provision coding.
Their effect is not a generic ``environmental provisions matter'' result: it is driven
specifically by the subset of agreements containing explicitly trade-liberalizing or
trade-restrictive environmental clauses, identified separately from cooperation-only
language because their 680-agreement panel has enough cross-agreement variation in
provision \emph{type} to support that decomposition (maximum VIF 4.6 across
provision-type variables). The Chinese PTAs of 2000--2015 are, on this same coding
scheme, overwhelmingly the cooperation-only type: mechanism-bearing content --- the exact
category driving Brandi et al.'s result --- appears in only 2 of the 14 agreements, and
is perfectly collinear across its two components in-sample. With that little variation in
provision type, this design cannot separately identify a mechanism-bearing effect even if
one exists, and a null on the aggregate index is what Brandi et al.'s own heterogeneity
would predict for a sample built almost entirely from cooperation-only chapters. The two
results are therefore complementary rather than contradictory: theirs shows that
mechanism-bearing provisions move trade when agreements contain them in sufficient
number and variety; this paper shows that China's agreements, over this period, did not
contain them.

A second, compounding difference is the level of aggregation at which the two designs
operate. \citet{brandi2020} work with UN Comtrade flows aggregated to the
exporter--importer--year level, with dirty/green export shares as the outcome; a
composition effect there can arise simply because the set of firms or products exporting
changes, without any single firm reallocating its own mix. The data used here are
firm--product(HS6)--destination--year transactions, and the preferred specification
saturates firm$\times$destination$\times$year fixed effects, so the EP coefficients are
identified from within-firm reallocation across products at a given destination and year
--- a strictly more demanding test of the same composition hypothesis, and one on which a
country-pair-level aggregation could in principle mask heterogeneity that this design is
built to detect.

\subsection{Extensive margin}

Log-linear estimates condition on positive flows. If EPs created green trade at the
extensive margin --- new firm--product--destination combinations --- the intensive
estimates would miss it \citep{santossilva2006, larch2025}. On a zero-filled
HS6--destination--year grid (8.3 million cells), PPML estimates of
equation~\eqref{eq:main}'s aggregated analogue confirm the null: EP$\times$green is
$+0.0015$ ($p=0.74$) with the WB index and $+0.0001$ ($p=0.95$) with TREND;
EP$\times$dirty is $-0.030$ ($p=0.06$) and $+0.003$ ($p=0.55$) respectively. There is
no green trade creation at the extensive margin, and the dirty-margin coefficient here
is, if anything, marginally stronger than in the intensive-margin specifications ---
consistent with the fragile-not-null reading of Section~\ref{sec:dirty}, though it does
not survive the conventional 5\% threshold on this grid either.

\subsection{Within-firm reallocation}

The margin that aggregate data cannot see: does the green \emph{share} of a
multiproduct firm's basket toward a destination respond to EP depth? On the
firm--destination--year panel of green shares (13.3 million observations; FE:
firm--destination and year; destination-clustered s.e.), the coefficient on WB EP
depth is $-0.0001$ ($p=0.50$) and on the TREND count $-0.00006$ ($p=0.043$,
asymptotic). Both are economically negligible: a one-standard-deviation increase in
the TREND count moves the within-firm green share by three hundredths of a percentage
point, with the ``wrong'' sign, and the marginal asymptotic significance is subject to
the same few-clusters caveat as everything else in this setting. Chinese firms did not
rebalance their baskets. This module speaks to the within-plant evidence of
\citet{cherniwchan2017}: where NAFTA tariff cuts changed what plants do, Chinese EP
chapters did not change what exporters sell.

\subsection{Sample robustness}

Table~\ref{tab:robust} collects the full-panel sample variations. Adding controls (MFN
tariff, market concentration, antidumping exposure) moves the green coefficient to an
exact zero ($-0.0002$, $p=0.94$). Excluding the eleven ASEAN destinations --- the block
that dominates the treated count, and whose single 2005 accession date means the
pooled coefficient could in principle be answering an ASEAN-specific rather than a
general question --- leaves it at $-0.0025$ ($p=0.48$): the estimate is not an artifact
of how heavily that one bloc weights the aggregation. Re-including
Hong Kong and Macao gives $-0.0009$ ($p=0.80$). Restricting to common-support products
gives $-0.0022$ ($p=0.57$). Across every variation the dirty coefficient sits between
$-0.0040$ and $-0.0060$ with asymptotic p-values between 0.02 and 0.09 --- persistently
marginal, and, as Section~\ref{sec:dirty} established, only partially robust to
bootstrap, permutation, and leave-one-out inference.

\subsection{Destination-specific trends in the composition gap}

The conservative-bias argument of Section~\ref{sec:strategy} has a testable
counterpart, and it is the paper's central falsification exercise: if destinations
signing deep environmental chapters have green demand rising for reasons of their own
--- a rival explanation for the null result, not merely a robustness formality ---
allowing each destination its own linear trend in the green (and dirty) gap, estimated
from data the treatment cannot have contaminated, should matter. Two variants of this exercise, run on the
collapsed panel, bracket the answer. The first adds destination-specific linear trends
interacted with green and dirty status, estimated over the full sample period. The WB
results are essentially unchanged (EP$\times$green $-0.0051$, bootstrap $p=0.50$;
EP$\times$dirty $-0.0082$, bootstrap $p=0.28$), but the TREND green interaction flips
sign relative to the baseline (from $+0.0018$, $p=0.32$, to $-0.0022$) and --- the only
coefficient in the paper's destination-trend and robustness battery to do so ---
survives the wild cluster bootstrap ($p=0.012$). Taken at face value it would say
environmental provisions \emph{reduce} green exports. It should not be taken at face
value: unit-specific trends estimated over the full sample are known to absorb
post-treatment dynamics and can reverse the sign of treatment coefficients when
effects or cohort dynamics are present \citep{wolfers2006}, and the negative late
drift of the green gap that these trends pick up is precisely the early-cohort
artifact that the Sun--Abraham estimator already dismissed
(Appendix~\ref{app:sunab}). The second variant removes the contamination by
construction: destination-specific trends are estimated on \emph{pre-treatment} years
only (all years for never-treated destinations) and projected forward before
re-estimating the main specification on the detrended outcome. Every coefficient
returns to an imprecise zero (TREND$\times$green $+0.0074$, bootstrap $p=0.18$;
WB$\times$green $+0.0168$, bootstrap $p=0.71$; dirty interactions $p=0.30$--$0.62$).
That the one nominally robust coefficient in the trend specification reverses sign
entirely depending on how the trends are estimated identifies it as an artifact of the
trend specification, not a treatment effect; the composition null stands against
destination-specific linear drifts in green demand, the most plausible form of the
selection-on-trends confounder.

\subsection{A continuous dirty margin: CO\textsubscript{2} intensity}

The binary dirty indicator groups HS6 products into the six classic
pollution-intensive sectors of \citet{mani1998}; a natural concern is that a
continuous, externally sourced measure of pollution intensity might tell a different
story. I construct one from the replication data of \citet{shapiro2021}, which
reports China-specific CO\textsubscript{2} emission intensity (direct and total,
i.e.\ including upstream inputs) for 47 EXIOBASE industries. Each industry code
encodes a two-digit ISIC Rev.\ 3 / NACE Rev.\ 1.1 division (verified against the
subset of industries the replication package labels by name: e.g.\ \emph{Pulp}
loads on division 21, \emph{Coke oven products} on division 23, \emph{Cement, lime
and plaster} on division 26, \emph{Iron and steel} and \emph{Aluminum} on division
27 --- exactly the ISIC divisions underlying the six Mani-Wheeler sectors). HS6
products are mapped to ISIC3 divisions via the WITS HS1996--ISIC3 concordance
already used for the binary classification, and assigned the simple average
China-specific intensity across the EXIOBASE sub-industries in their division (no
sub-industry weights are available in the replication data); unmatched HS6 codes
(9.5\% of the panel) are assigned the sample mean. The resulting measure validates
against the existing trichotomy without circularity, since it is built independently
of the green/dirty/neutral classification: mean intensity is $0.0012$ for dirty
products, $0.0008$ for neutral products, and $0.0006$ for green products ---
monotonic in the expected direction.

Re-estimating equation~\eqref{eq:main} on the collapsed panel with EP interacted with
the standardized continuous intensity in place of the binary dirty indicator gives
the same reading as the rest of the paper: EP$\times$intensity is $-0.0025$ with the
WB index (bootstrap $p=0.71$) and $-0.0013$ with TREND (asymptotic $p=0.012$,
bootstrap $p=0.06$) --- a fade under robust inference consistent with the marginal,
non-robust pattern documented throughout Section~\ref{sec:dirty}, not a new or
sharper result. The continuous measure does not overturn the conclusion that the
composition null is not an artifact of how dirty products are classified.

% ============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================================

Using the universe of Chinese customs transactions and two independent codings of the
environmental content of China's trade agreements, this paper finds that environmental
provisions did not change what China exports on the green margin, the extensive margin,
or the within-firm basket: a precise null, stable across estimation designs and sample
variations, that survives an inference battery designed for the few-treated-clusters
regime that characterizes this --- and most --- PTA settings. The dirty margin is the
one partial exception: a negative effect on dirty exports survives some but not all of
the same battery, and is driven by a single destination (Section~\ref{sec:dirty}) ---
suggestive of a real, if narrowly identified, effect rather than a clean artifact, and a
result the paper reports as fragile rather than either confirmed or dismissed.

The reading is not that environmental provisions never matter: \citet{abman2024} show
they can, on environmental outcomes, when they are specific and enforceable. The reading
is that the environmental chapters China signed in 2000--2015 --- thin, bundled, and
dominated by cooperation language --- had no trade bite. For the policy debate on
greening trade agreements, the distinction matters: content design, not chapter
presence, is what could move flows. For the empirical literature, the paper adds a
cautionary methodological tale: with treatment varying across a dozen agreements,
asymptotically significant composition effects appear and dissolve under honest
inference; the discipline of bootstrap, permutation, and leave-one-out should be
standard equipment.

\appendix
\section{Sun--Abraham dynamics on the composition gap}\label{app:sunab}
% ============================================================================

Figure~\ref{fig:sunab} plots the interaction-weighted event study of
\citet{sun2021} estimated on the destination--year composition gaps (the difference
between the weighted mean outcome of green, respectively dirty, products and that of
neutral products within each destination--year), with never-treated destinations as
the control group and $t=-1$ as the reference period. The aggregated ATTs confirm the
static results: $-0.044$ ($p=0.24$) for the green gap and $+0.073$ ($p=0.28$) for the
dirty gap.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/eventstudy_sunab.png}
\caption{Sun--Abraham event study on the destination--year composition gap, window
$[-6,+5]$; destination and year fixed effects, cell-count weights,
destination-clustered 95\% bands; $t=-1$ is the reference period.}
\label{fig:sunab}
\end{figure}

One coefficient in the pre-period deserves explicit discussion: the dirty gap at
$t=-6$ is $+0.047$ with an asymptotic $p$-value of $0.001$. Taken at face value it
would qualify the flat-pre-trend reading of Section~\ref{sec:results}. A cohort
decomposition shows it should not be taken at face value, for four reasons.

First, distant leads are identified by very few units: the sample starts in 2000, so
$t=-6$ exists only for the eight destinations that entered in 2006 or later (Chile,
Pakistan, New Zealand, Peru, Costa Rica, Iceland, Switzerland, Australia); deeper
leads rest on as few as a single destination.

Second, the cohort-specific estimates underlying the aggregate are mutually
contradictory: at $t=-6$ the 2006 cohort (Chile) deviates by $-0.49$ and the 2010
cohort (Peru) by $-0.33$, against $+0.14$ for the 2008 cohort (New Zealand) and
$+0.10$ for the 2015 cohort (Australia). There is no common displacement of dirty
exports ahead of the agreements --- only destination-specific fluctuations of opposite
sign, averaged into a single number with trade-size weights.

Third, the aggregate is not robust to any single cohort: dropping the 2015 cohort
(Australia alone, whose $t=-6$ falls in 2009) flips the coefficient to $-0.051$
($p=0.11$); dropping the 2014--15 cohorts yields $-0.109$. A genuine pre-trend does
not change sign when one destination is removed. Nor does it appear only at one
isolated lead: the adjacent leads $t=-5$ through $t=-2$, where 17 to 23 destinations
are observed, are all small and statistically indistinguishable from zero, and the
two-way fixed-effects event study of Figure~\ref{fig:es} shows nothing at $t=-6$
either ($+0.027$, $p=0.53$).

Fourth, the design estimates roughly 28 lead and lag coefficients with 23 treated
clusters, so the clustered variance matrix is rank-deficient and individual-lead
$p$-values are unreliable by construction; with this many coefficients, one or two
nominally significant leads are what sampling noise delivers under exactly flat
pre-trends. The aggregated ATT --- the statistic with a valid interpretation ---
remains null in every variant: $p=0.27$ in the baseline, $p=0.11$ restricting to the
$[-6,+5]$ window, $p=0.29$ excluding the 2014--15 cohorts.
