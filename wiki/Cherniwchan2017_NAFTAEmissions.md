# Trade Liberalization and the Environment: Evidence from NAFTA and U.S. Manufacturing

**Cherniwchan (2017)**
*Journal of International Economics*, 105, 130–149
[Paper Link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2017.01.005)

---

## Abstract

The unobserved responses of individual polluters are often used to rationalize the aggregate effects of international trade on the environment. In this paper, I provide the first evidence of these responses. I estimate the effects of NAFTA on the emissions of particulate matter (PM10) and sulfur dioxide (SO2) from manufacturing plants in the United States. My findings suggest that trade liberalization led to significant reductions of these pollutants at affected plants. On average, nearly two-thirds of the reductions in PM10 and SO2 emissions from the U.S. manufacturing sector between 1994 and 1998 can be attributed to trade liberalization following NAFTA.

---

## Research Question

What happens *within plants* when trade is liberalized? Aggregate trade-environment correlations confound entry/exit, reallocation, and within-unit changes; this paper isolates the within-plant (technique) margin using NAFTA tariff cuts as the shock.

---

## Results

- NAFTA tariff reductions caused significant **within-plant** reductions in PM10 and SO2 emissions in US manufacturing.
- ~2/3 of the total decline in US manufacturing PM10/SO2 emissions 1994–1998 is attributable to post-NAFTA liberalization.
- Demonstrates that the *technique effect* operates at the micro level, not just via composition across industries.

---

## Data

- US plant-level emissions (EPA registries) linked to manufacturing plants.
- Industry-level US and Mexican tariff changes under NAFTA.
- Panel of plants around NAFTA entry into force (1994).

---

## Methodology

- Difference-in-differences exploiting cross-industry variation in NAFTA tariff cuts (own-tariff and export-market-tariff exposure), with plant fixed effects.
- Decomposition of aggregate emission changes into entry/exit, reallocation, and within-plant components.
