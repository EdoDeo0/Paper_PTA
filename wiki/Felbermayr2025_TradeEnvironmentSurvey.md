# Trade and the Environment, Trade Policies and Environmental Policies — How Do They Interact? (2025)

Felbermayr et al. (2025) — Gabriel Felbermayr, Sonja Peterson, Joschka Wanner
*Journal of Economic Surveys*, Vol. 39, No. 3, pp. 1148–1184
[Paper Link](https://doi.org/10.1111/joes.12628)

## Abstract

While international trade can offer gains from specialization and access to a wider range of products, it is also closely interlinked with global environmental problems, above all, anthropogenic climate change. This survey provides a structured overview of the economic literature on the interaction between environmental outcomes, trade, environmental policy and trade policy. It covers approaches ranging from descriptive data analysis based on input-output tables, over quantitative trade models and econometric studies, to game-theoretic analyses. Addressed issues include in particular the emission content of trade and emissions along value chains, the relocation of dirty firms and environmental impacts abroad, impacts of specific trade policies (such as trade agreements or tariffs) or environmental policies (such as border carbon adjustment), transportation emissions, as well as the role of firms. Across the different topics covered, the paper also tries to identify avenues for future research.

## Research Question

How does the economic literature characterize the interaction between trade, environmental outcomes, trade policy, and environmental policy? Broad survey paper, no primary identification. Synthesizes findings across sub-literatures including: emissions embodied in trade, pollution havens, trade agreements and the environment, carbon border adjustment mechanisms (CBAM), and firm-level responses.

## Results

Key findings from the survey: (1) trade shifts the location of emissions but the net global effect is ambiguous and depends on trade costs and environmental policy asymmetries; (2) pollution haven effects exist but are quantitatively smaller than often assumed; (3) trade agreements can promote or reduce environmental protection depending on their content and enforcement — effects are heterogeneous and the literature is not settled; (4) border carbon adjustment mechanisms (e.g. the EU CBAM) can reduce carbon leakage but raise issues of WTO compatibility and distributional effects; (5) firm-level heterogeneity matters greatly for both trade and emissions outcomes.

## Data

Survey paper; no primary dataset. Reviews quantitative trade models (gravity, general equilibrium), econometric studies, and game-theoretic analyses. Key databases referenced include TREND (environmental provisions), DESTA (PTA depth), World Input-Output Database (WIOD), and various national customs and firm datasets.

## Methodology

Structured literature survey organized by topic. The paper covers: (i) emission content of trade and global value chains; (ii) pollution haven hypothesis and carbon leakage; (iii) trade policy effects on the environment (PTAs, tariffs, subsidies); (iv) environmental policy effects on trade (CBAM, carbon taxes); (v) transportation emissions; (vi) firm-level trade and emissions. Each section synthesizes empirical and theoretical findings and identifies open questions for future research.

## **Why relevant for Paper_PTA**

Useful as a general-framing citation for the broader trade-environment nexus when introducing the literature in Section 1 or the related literature section. Less targeted than Gutsch et al. (2024) for the specific claim about EP effectiveness in PTAs being contested — Felbermayr covers that topic among many others. Best used to motivate the broad question rather than to document the fragmented state of the EP-specific literature.
