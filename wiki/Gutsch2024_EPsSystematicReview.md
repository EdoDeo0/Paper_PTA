# Effects of Environmental Provisions in International Trade Agreements on Businesses and Economies: A Systematic Review (2024)

Gutsch et al. (2024) — Michelle Gutsch, Johanna Mai, Nelli Ukhova, Samanthi Dijkstra-Silva
*Sustainability Accounting, Management and Policy Journal*, Vol. 16, No. 7, pp. 1–27
[Paper Link](https://doi.org/10.1108/SAMPJ-02-2024-0122)

## Abstract

International trade and its effects on the environment are increasingly discussed both in academia and by policymakers. To counter negative effects of international trade on the environment, so-called environmental provisions have been integrated in trade agreements aimed at businesses and economies. However, as both the intent and effectiveness of these provisions are controversial, this paper aims to provide an overview of the current state of research and identify key factors influencing their effectiveness. The authors conduct a systematic literature review of 44 papers on the effects of environmental provisions in international trade agreements on the environmental performance of businesses, as well as economies, from 1992 to mid-2024. The integration of environmental provisions into trade agreements generally has positive effects on the environment while their effectiveness is influenced by their design and several boundary conditions. Alongside the importance of effective enforcement mechanisms, reputation concerns of businesses and lobbyism are discussed as intermediary factors in the design and impact of environmental provisions.

## Research Question

What does the existing empirical literature say about the effects of environmental provisions in trade agreements — on both environmental outcomes and economic/trade outcomes — at the macro (country/region) and micro (firm/business) levels? No primary identification strategy; this is a systematic literature review of 44 papers (1992–mid 2024), following PRISMA protocol.

## Results

The overall picture from 44 studies is mixed. On environmental outcomes, EPs tend to have positive effects — reducing emissions, slowing deforestation, improving air quality — but effectiveness is highly conditional on provision design (enforceable vs. aspirational), enforcement mechanisms, and country context. On economic/trade outcomes, results are "fragmented and controversial": some studies find that EPs increase or have no effect on trade flows, others find trade-restricting effects (especially for developing-country exports to high-income markets). Micro-level effects on firms are underexplored. The review explicitly concludes that "the current academic literature is fragmented," with no simple generalizable answer to whether EPs work.

## Data

Systematic review; no primary dataset. 44 empirical papers selected from databases including Web of Science, Scopus, and EconLit, covering 1992 to mid-2024. Studies cover a wide range of methodologies (gravity models, DiD, synthetic controls, firm surveys) and outcomes (CO₂ emissions, trade volumes, domestic environmental legislation, deforestation, firm behavior).

## Methodology

PRISMA-compliant systematic literature review. Papers selected based on pre-defined inclusion criteria: must study effects of EPs in bilateral or multilateral trade agreements; must include quantitative or qualitative empirical evidence; must be peer-reviewed. Studies coded by: level of analysis (macro/micro), type of outcome (environmental/economic), provision type, geographic scope, and findings direction. No meta-analysis or quantitative pooling of estimates.

## **Why relevant for Paper_PTA**

This is the most direct citation available for the claim that *whether environmental provisions in trade agreements have a genuine effect on trade flows and composition remains an open and contested empirical question*. The review explicitly uses the word "fragmented" and documents that the literature is split, making it ideal as a supporting reference for framing a null result.
