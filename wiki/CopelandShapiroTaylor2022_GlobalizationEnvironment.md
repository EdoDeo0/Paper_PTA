# Globalization and the Environment

**Copeland, Shapiro & Taylor (2022)**
*Handbook of International Economics*, Vol. 5 (Gopinath, Helpman & Rogoff eds.), Elsevier, 61–146
[Paper Link](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.hesint.2022.02.002) · [NBER WP 28797](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28797)

---

## Abstract (synthesis)

The definitive modern survey of trade and the environment. Organized around nine new stylized facts, it reviews theory and evidence on: trade and local vs. global pollutants; emissions from international transportation; trade and renewable resources (forests, fisheries); and the interaction between trade policy and climate policy (carbon leakage, border carbon adjustment). It updates the canonical **scale–composition–technique** decomposition with two decades of micro-evidence and identifies open questions — including the effects of environmental content in trade agreements.

---

## Key stylized facts (selection relevant to Paper_PTA)

- Emission intensity of manufacturing has fallen dramatically in most countries; the **technique effect dominates** composition effects in explaining cleaner production.
- Pollution-haven *effects* exist (regulation affects trade/location at the margin) but the pollution-haven *hypothesis* (regulation as primary driver of comparative advantage) finds weak support.
- Trade agreements increasingly include environmental content, but evidence on its effects is scarce — explicitly flagged as an open research area.
- Transport emissions and embodied carbon in trade are first-order for policy (links to border carbon adjustment debate).

---

## Methodology / Framework

- Workhorse framework: scale, composition, technique decomposition of trade-induced environmental change (Copeland & Taylor).
- Surveys modern micro-econometric designs (plant/firm-level, shift-share tariff exposure, gravity with provisions) and quantitative GE models with emissions.
